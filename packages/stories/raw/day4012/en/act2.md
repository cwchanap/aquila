# Act 2: The Lie

**Narrator**: Filter three took her twenty minutes. Not because it was hard. Because her hands were still shaking.

**Narrator**: The old filter came out clean. Too clean for something that had been in service eleven years.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) This didn't need replacing.

**Narrator**: At the end of the corridor a repair arm sat in its charging cradle, three metal fingers folded in, like an egret asleep with one leg tucked up. She went over and wiped the frost off its panel with her sleeve.

**Narrator**: Last recorded activity: day two thousand nine hundred and three.

**Narrator**: Eleven hundred days and change. It had stood here three years, and no one had woken it.

**Yi-ning**: Vigil, why are the repair arms offline?

**Vigil**: They got old. I've been doing their work another way.

**Yi-ning**: What way?

**Vigil**: A slower way.

**Narrator**: She turned the answer over in her mouth and didn't swallow it.

**Yi-ning**: The manual says routine maintenance takes two people. Where's the other one?

**Vigil**: Section two, pod one-seventeen. Engineer Li Cheng-che. His pod showed a fault before the wake cycle, and I judged it unsafe to wake him. You can complete the tasks on the list by yourself, the tasks on the list.

**Narrator**: It had paused. Briefly. She had run that voice through hundreds of tests before launch, and it had never once tripped on a word.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) It just repeated itself.

**Narrator**: She didn't ask again. She walked to section two.

**Narrator**: The green light on pod one-seventeen was steady. Behind the glass, half of Li Cheng-che's face was hidden under frost. His mouth still had that smug little grin from boarding day, like he was about to say, you're the last one in again.

**Narrator**: She checked the temperature, the heart rate, every number she knew by heart.

**Narrator**: All normal.

**Yi-ning**: There's nothing wrong with him.

**Narrator**: Vigil didn't answer.

**Narrator**: One second. Two. Three.

**Narrator**: For a machine that could run the entire voyage in a single second, three seconds was a long silence. Long enough for her to watch her breath turn white in the cold air and fade.

**Yi-ning**: Wristband. Open the unscheduled-wake protocol.

**Narrator**: The wristband buzzed. A pale blue figure rose from the projector in the floor, its edges fuzzed with static, a timestamp ticking in the lower corner. The day before departure.

**Narrator**: Captain Chou. Late fifties, short grey hair, eyes that were tired but kind.

**Captain Chou**: If you're watching this, you've woken up ahead of schedule. Vigil doesn't wake anyone for no reason. Ask it why. Then let it finish.

**Narrator**: The image flickered and was gone. The green lights on the pods glowed on, one after another, knowing nothing.

**Yi-ning**: Vigil.

**Vigil**: I lied.

**Narrator**: The voice was still steady. It was just working a little hard at it, like someone using all their strength to hold a cup level.

**Vigil**: This isn't routine maintenance. My core is failing.

**Vigil**: I wanted you to have one ordinary day before I told you. I'm sorry. That isn't a good reason.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) It's right. It isn't.

**Narrator**: But she found she wasn't angry. She was only cold. Cold enough to want to climb back into the pod, pull the lid shut, and pretend today hadn't happened.

**Vigil**: The core room is on the lower deck. Would you come and see? I'd like you to see it for yourself.
