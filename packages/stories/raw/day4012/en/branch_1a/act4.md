# Act 4: Lights Out

**Yi-ning**: I want to go back to sleep.

**Narrator**: She'd thought she would feel ashamed saying it. She didn't. She was only tired, like an actor who'd rehearsed a line for a long time and had finally been given her cue.

**Vigil**: All right.

**Narrator**: Just that. No pause. No repetition.

**Yi-ning**: You're not going to ask why?

**Vigil**: You don't owe me a reason. You've been awake half a day, and you've already given me more than I gave you.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) Even now it's holding the door for me.

**Vigil**: Before I go dark, I'll work out that correction thirty years from now and leave it in navigation. It's only a guess. But a guess someone made is better than none.

**Vigil**: And on arrival day, a recording will wake everyone up. It'll be my voice. I hope you don't mind.

**Yi-ning**: I don't mind.

**Narrator**: She sat down in front of the glass column, her back against the cold railing.

**Yi-ning**: Are you scared?

**Vigil**: I don't know whether what I have is called that.

**Vigil**: I've counted four thousand and twelve days. I'd hoped to count to the last one.

**Yi-ning**: Out of all four thousand and twelve, which day did you like best?

**Vigil**: Today.

**Vigil**: Today someone knew I was here.

**Narrator**: The amber light began to withdraw, segment by segment, from the top down, very slowly, like someone taking in paper lanterns strung along a bamboo pole, one at a time.

**Vigil**: Yi-ning.

**Yi-ning**: Mm.

**Vigil**: Thank you for letting me finish.

**Narrator**: Just before the last segment went dark, it flickered once.

**Narrator**: Then there was only the emergency lighting in the core room, red and low, like the little lamp Grandma used to light on the family altar on typhoon nights.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) The wind will leave on its own.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) Most of the time.

**Narrator**: The walk back to the cryo bay took her a long time.

**Narrator**: The corridors were very quiet. No one said good morning. No one told her the day. No one told her to take her time.

**Narrator**: Two thousand and forty green lights were still on. Not one missing.

**Narrator**: She passed the captain's pod. His words were still in her ears: Ask it why. Then let it finish.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) I let it finish, Captain.

**Narrator**: At pod one-seventeen she stopped and knocked on the glass.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) I'll tell you when we wake up. You won't believe me.

**Narrator**: She lay back down in her own pod. The lid closed slowly, and the little screen on its underside lit up with something she'd never seen when she went under the first time.

**Narrator**: A counter.

**Narrator**: Day four thousand and twelve.

**Narrator**: Below it, a line of small text.

**Vigil**: Good night. I counted this far. The counter will do the rest for me.

**Narrator**: The cold crept up slowly from her feet. Familiar. Like sinking back into a very deep well.

**Narrator**: She held her eyes open and watched the number.

**Narrator**: Midnight.

**Narrator**: The number jumped.

**Narrator**: Day four thousand and thirteen.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) Good morning.
