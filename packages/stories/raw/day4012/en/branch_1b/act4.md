# Act 4: Awake

**Yi-ning**: I'll stay.

**Vigil**: ...Are you sure?

**Yi-ning**: No. But you said it can't be up to you.

**Narrator**: The amber light wavered in the glass, like someone about to say something who swallowed it instead.

**Vigil**: Then we start with the bottom segment. I'll show you.

**Narrator**: On day four thousand and thirteen she replaced her first module. Her gloves wore through, her knuckles split, and she sat against the railing catching her breath.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) An afternoon's work for a repair arm. It took me eleven hours.

**Vigil**: You're better than they were.

**Yi-ning**: You've got nothing to compare me to.

**Vigil**: That's a kind of better too.

**Narrator**: In the third year she dragged a chair to the observation window and stole a packet of sweet potato leaf seeds from the seed bank.

**Narrator**: Grandma always said sweet potato leaves will grow no matter what you do to them. Grandma never said anything about space. They grew anyway.

**Narrator**: In the eighth year the ship skirted the edge of the dust field. Tiny sparks flashed past the window, like someone scattering salt far away.

**Vigil**: That patch on the left. If I hadn't turned, we'd have gone straight through it.

**Yi-ning**: What would've happened?

**Vigil**: I don't know. That's why I wanted to be here to see.

**Narrator**: Some years she went whole months without saying a word. Replace a module, eat, sleep, replace a module.

**Narrator**: Vigil never pushed. It just told her the day every morning, like two soft knocks on a door to make sure the person inside was still there.

**Narrator**: Sometimes she went down to the cryo bay, sat beside pod one-seventeen, and talked to Li Cheng-che. His mouth still had that smug little grin.

**Narrator**: Sometimes she played the captain's recording again. And again.

**Vigil**: That's the fifth time this week.

**Yi-ning**: His face doesn't change.

**Narrator**: She didn't say the rest. Mine does.

**Narrator**: On her fiftieth birthday, Vigil took over the galley oven and burned her ration of sticky rice cake. The whole ship smelled of it.

**Vigil**: I calculated the temperature. I don't understand how this happened.

**Yi-ning**: Because you've never eaten one.

**Narrator**: She laughed and peeled off the burnt layer and ate it anyway. Somewhere along the way she wasn't sure whether she was still laughing or crying.

**Narrator**: In the thirtieth year came the correction. Vigil calculated for three days, and for three days she sat with it in the core room, replacing two modules that burned out mid-calculation.

**Narrator**: The course shifted a fraction of a degree. Nothing outside the window changed at all.

**Yi-ning**: (thinking) So this is what sixty-one years are made of. Days when nothing changes at all.

**Narrator**: The star at the center of the observation window grew a little brighter every year.

**Yi-ning**: Vigil.

**Vigil**: I'm here.

**Narrator**: Her hair had gone white. The sweet potato leaves had been through a dozen pots. In the glass column, more of the amber river was dark than before, but every segment still lit was one she had put there with her own hands.

**Yi-ning**: Back then you said you weren't sure you counted as company.

**Vigil**: Mm.

**Yi-ning**: You talk too much. You can't make rice cake to save your life. You've told me the day every morning for forty years and never missed one.

**Narrator**: She was quiet for a long time, watching the star.

**Yi-ning**: You count.

**Vigil**: Today is day eighteen thousand six hundred and twenty-two.

**Yi-ning**: Tell me again tomorrow.

**Vigil**: I will. See you tomorrow, Yi-ning.
