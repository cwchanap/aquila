# Act 1: The Lobby at Two A.M.

**Narrator**: 1:58 a.m.

**Narrator**: Only half the lobby lights were on. The security desk sat empty, a faded jacket slung over the back of the chair, the radio left on, playing old songs at a murmur, like someone talking to themselves in the next room.

**Narrator**: The fluorescent tube in the ceiling hummed without pause, flickered once, held. The mailbox wall was plastered with stickers for drain cleaners and moving companies, each layer over the last, like the building's tree rings.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Forty-year-old building. Probably forty-year-old light bulbs.

**Fang Rui**: Are you there yet?

**Narrator**: Fang Rui's voice came down from the ninth floor, through several floors of concrete and one phone, and sounded farther away than usual.

**Xu Nian**: Yeah. Nobody here.

**Fang Rui**: Obviously. Who's in a lobby at two a.m.?

**Narrator**: On the other end, the rustle of a blanket. Xu Nian could almost see her—curled up on their secondhand sofa in the living room, hugging a cushion, half scared, half thrilled.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) The one who suggested it is hiding under a blanket. The one doing it is standing in the lobby. Seems fair.

**Narrator**: It had started at eleven. Halfway through their final papers, neither of them wanting to move, Fang Rui scrolled into a post titled "The Elevator Game, Tested." Hundreds of comments underneath. Half said it was fake. Half said don't.

**Fang Rui**: Didn't you say you're not scared of anything?

**Xu Nian**: I said I'm not scared of ghosts. I'm scared of final papers.

**Fang Rui**: Perfect. Come back from this and the paper won't scare you anymore.

**Narrator**: She said it easily, like she was talking about running downstairs for a late-night snack.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Really, I just can't sleep. Bed at three or bed at five—the only difference is two more hours of ceiling.

**Narrator**: Xu Nian pressed the call button.

**Narrator**: The floor display was the old kind, red digits, counting down from eight, hesitating before every number. Cables dragged through the shaft, a long, heavy sound, like something turning over inside the wall.

**Fang Rui**: Okay, rules one more time. One: you ride alone. If someone tries to get on partway, you get off and try another night.

**Fang Rui**: Two: press four, two, six, two, ten. Don't get off on any floor. Wait for the doors to close before you press the next one.

**Fang Rui**: Three: on ten, press five. On five, a woman might get on. Don't talk to her. Don't look at her face.

**Xu Nian**: What if she talks to me first?

**Fang Rui**: ...It doesn't say.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Very thorough.

**Fang Rui**: Four: once she's on, press one. If the elevator goes down, it didn't work, go home and sleep. If it goes up—

**Narrator**: Fang Rui paused.

**Fang Rui**: Once you're on ten, the buttons stop working. If you want to come back, you can only press one. Only once. The last rule says: if you step out, don't look back.

**Narrator**: Ding.

**Narrator**: The doors slid apart. The left one always lagged half a beat, like an old man with a bad leg.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Grandma used to say: no mirrors after midnight, and if someone calls your name, don't turn around to answer. Grandma never said anything about elevators.

**Fang Rui**: Nian. Don't hang up, okay?

**Narrator**: Xu Nian stepped in.
