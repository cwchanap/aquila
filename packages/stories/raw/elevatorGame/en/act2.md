# Act 2: Four, Two, Six, Two, Ten

**Narrator**: The elevator was tiny. The plate said nine-person capacity; nine people in here would have to hug. The mirror on the back wall had been scratched in several places, and the inspection certificate was dated three years ago.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Don't look in the mirror. Not one of the rules. I'm adding it.

**Narrator**: She pressed four. The button lit with an orange ring, the car jolted, and began to climb.

**Narrator**: The fourth-floor corridor was dark except for the exit sign at the far end, a little green glow floating there. Outside one door sat a pair of men's slippers, toes pointing in, perfectly neat.

**Narrator**: The doors closed. Two.

**Narrator**: On the second floor, someone's TV was still on, canned laughter coming through an iron door in waves—laughter, then quiet, then laughter again.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Watching variety shows at two a.m. Healthier than me.

**Narrator**: Six.

**Narrator**: The sixth floor smelled of incense, faint, mixed with damp walls. In the few seconds the doors stood open, slippers slapped against the floor somewhere deep down the hall, pat, pat, pat, coming this way—then the doors closed.

**Fang Rui**: It's so quiet where you are.

**Xu Nian**: Someone on six is burning incense.

**Fang Rui**: ...Don't do that to me.

**Xu Nian**: You're the one who sent me.

**Narrator**: Two.

**Narrator**: The TV was still on. The canned laughter rose—the same bit. Same pitch, same woman whose laugh cracked at the end, as if someone had rewound those ten seconds and played them again.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Variety shows get reruns. Totally normal.

**Narrator**: She pressed ten.

**Narrator**: The climb took longer than she remembered. Every time the red digits ticked over, they hesitated a little longer.

**Narrator**: At eight, the light overhead flickered. At nine, twice more—it dimmed, then dragged itself back on.

**Fang Rui**: Nian? What floor are you on?

**Narrator**: Fang Rui's voice arrived half a beat late, as if rising from deep underwater.

**Xu Nian**: Ten.

**Narrator**: The doors opened on ten. It was dark out there, too dark to tell how long the corridor was. Only a slow draft of cold air flowing in, pressing against her ankles.

**Narrator**: She pressed five. Only when the doors closed did she realize she hadn't been breathing.

**Fang Rui**: ...Ten... you said... ten?

**Narrator**: Static hissed between every word, like a tape that had gotten damp.

**Narrator**: Nine. Eight. Seven. Six.

**Narrator**: Five.

**Narrator**: Ding.

**Narrator**: First, cold. The cold after rain, wet, smelling of asphalt and rust. Then a pair of shoes, black flats, soaked through, stepping into the elevator without a sound.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Don't look up.

**Narrator**: The car sank slightly, as if it had taken on another person's weight. Only slightly. Less than a person.

**Narrator**: She stared at her sneakers. Her left lace had come undone. She didn't dare bend down to tie it. At the edge of her vision, the flats had stopped in the corner, toes pointed at her.

**Narrator**: A long strand of hair hung down, its ends dripping. One drop hit the floor. Then another.

**Fang Rui**: Nian? Did someone get on? Say something.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Don't talk to her. What about the person on the phone? It doesn't say.

**Narrator**: She bit her lip. Her finger hovered over the panel for a second, then pressed one.

**Narrator**: The doors closed. The car began to move.

**Narrator**: Up.

**Narrator**: Six. Seven. Eight. Nine.
