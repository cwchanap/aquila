# Act 3: The Tenth Floor

**Narrator**: Ten.

**Narrator**: When the doors opened, the light changed.

**Narrator**: It didn't go out. It went red. The tube was still on, but the light came through as if through red cellophane, dyeing the mirror, the buttons, her hands, all one color.

**Narrator**: Outside was a corridor. Identical to the one on her own ninth floor—same terrazzo, same iron doors, same row of shoe cabinets. Except there was no sound. No air-conditioner compressors, no distant scooters, no one's fridge humming. Quiet like a hand cupped over her ears.

**Narrator**: The window at the end of the corridor showed no night view. Only an even, unbroken red.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Fine. Close the doors.

**Narrator**: She pressed door-close. It didn't light. Twelve, seven, nine—every one like pressing a wall. The doors just stayed open, like a mouth, waiting for her to walk out on her own.

**Narrator**: Behind her, the woman didn't move. Water hit the floor, one drop, then another, slow, like a clock.

**Fang Rui**: Nian? Are you still there?

**Narrator**: Suddenly the line was clear. Unnaturally clear, Fang Rui's voice right against her ear, thick, like she'd been crying.

**Fang Rui**: Say something, please? Where are you?

**Narrator**: Xu Nian opened her mouth.

**Narrator**: Someone behind her spoke first.

**The Fifth-Floor Woman**: I'm here.

**Narrator**: It was her voice.

**Narrator**: Not like it. Exactly it. A little rough, dropping at the end of the sentence—the voice Fang Rui said sounded like she never got enough sleep. From half a step behind her, close enough that the breath stirred the hair on her neck.

**Fang Rui**: You made it? Ground floor? God, you scared me... Okay, wait for me, I'm coming down to get you.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) That's not me. Rui, that's not me.

**Narrator**: The line went dead. Beep, beep, beep, and then not even that.

**Narrator**: On the panel, only one button's number was still legible. One. Pressed for forty years, worn almost smooth, in the red light it looked like a small, faded exit.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) If you want to come back, you can only press one. Only once.

**Narrator**: But Fang Rui had said the ground floor. That voice said "I'm here," and Fang Rui believed it, and right now she was probably shuffling down the stairs in her slippers to meet someone who wasn't Xu Nian.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) And if I press one and nothing happens? It doesn't say.

**Narrator**: She looked at the row of shoe cabinets outside. On the ninth floor, the third door down the corridor was home. If this place really was identical—

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Then I get there before she does.

**Narrator**: Outside the doors, the corridor waited quietly.

**Narrator**: Inside, the water kept dripping, one drop at a time, closer and closer to her feet.
