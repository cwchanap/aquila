# Act 4: The One Who Went Home

**Narrator**: She stepped out.

**Narrator**: The moment her foot touched the terrazzo, she realized—there was no sound. Not quiet. None. Rubber soles on stone should make a little scuff, a little echo, but there was nothing, like walking into a photograph.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Don't look back.

**Narrator**: Behind her, the elevator doors began to close. That sound she could hear: the left door half a beat behind, clunk, clunk, shut.

**Narrator**: Then the cables. Long and heavy. Going down.

**Narrator**: The iron doors on both sides were closed, their numbers eaten by the red light, leaving only blank patches. She walked past them one by one, and then she stopped.

**Narrator**: The third door. A grey doormat, one corner dented where the washing machine had sat on it. On the shoe cabinet, a pair of white canvas sneakers with the laces tucked inside—her habit, the one Fang Rui had nagged her about for a whole year. On the handle hung a dried-flower wreath Fang Rui had bought at a market, then realized at home it was an ugly color.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) Home. Ninth floor, number three.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) This is the tenth floor.

**Narrator**: There were sounds behind the door.

**Narrator**: The first sound she'd heard since setting foot in this corridor. Muffled through the iron, as if through water. Fang Rui laughing, still thick from crying.

**Fang Rui**: Do you know how scared I was? You stopped talking in the middle of the call, I waited in the lobby for ten minutes...

**Narrator**: Another voice answered her. A little rough, dropping at the end, like someone who never got enough sleep.

**Narrator**: "I'm back, aren't I?"

**Narrator**: Her own voice.

**Narrator**: She raised her hand and knocked. Knuckles against iron, hard enough to hurt the bone.

**Narrator**: No sound.

**Xu Nian**: Rui!

**Narrator**: She screamed it. She could feel her throat vibrate, feel every bit of air forced out of her lungs. But the corridor held nothing—as if the moment the name left her mouth, someone had caught it and taken it away.

**Fang Rui**: Why do you sound so weird?

**Narrator**: "Probably a cold," the voice said. "It was freezing in the elevator."

**Narrator**: Footsteps came up to the door.

**Narrator**: The peephole darkened. Someone was looking out from inside.

**Narrator**: Xu Nian stood in the red light, on her own doorstep, less than half a meter from that small circle of glass. She didn't step back. She didn't look back. She watched it go dark, and then light again.

**Narrator**: "No one," her voice said. "Probably next door."

**Narrator**: The footsteps moved away. The living-room TV came on, canned laughter rising in waves—laughter, then quiet, then laughter again.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) The same bit.

**Narrator**: The red light in the corridor didn't change. The window didn't change. Behind her, the direction she couldn't turn to look, nothing changed either.

**Narrator**: Somewhere far behind her, someone called softly.

**Narrator**: "Nian."

**Narrator**: It was Fang Rui's voice.

**Xu Nian**: (thinking) If someone calls your name, don't turn around to answer.

**Narrator**: She rested her forehead against the cold iron door.

**Narrator**: On the other side, someone said goodnight in her voice.
