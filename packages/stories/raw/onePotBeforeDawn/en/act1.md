# Act 1: The First Guest

**Narrator**: The rain had started at dusk. By the hour of the boar it showed no sign of stopping.

**Narrator**: Mountain rain doesn't fall. It comes sideways, gust after gust against the bamboo blinds, like someone outside impatiently turning pages.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man crouched by the red clay stove and fed it more charcoal. The water in the kettle had just begun to bubble, fine beads rising one at a time.

**Uncle Wen**: Crab eyes. Not yet.

**Narrator**: Her master didn't turn around. He sat behind the counter polishing an old teapot. He'd been at it nearly two hours. It had been shiny enough to see your face in for most of them.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) Everything he polishes tonight takes forever.

**Narrator**: The water's sound thickened. The bubbles grew, rolled up, and broke.

**Uncle Wen**: Fish eyes. Warm the pot.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man poured the boiling water over the pot. Steam plastered itself across her face. The pot was purple clay, older than she was, a hairline crack in the lid mended in gold, like a sleeping snake.

**Narrator**: Leaves in. Water in. Lid on. Count to five.

**Narrator**: The first infusion, poured away.

**Narrator**: The tea ran into the bamboo channel under the tray, amber with a touch of red.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) Such a waste. Every time.

**Uncle Wen**: A waste?

**Narrator**: Xiao Man jumped. She hadn't said a word.

**Uncle Wen**: Every time you pour off the first infusion, your mouth turns down. Seven years now.

**Uncle Wen**: Leaves get sunned, roasted, rolled, fired. They pick up dust, sweat, wind from the road. The first infusion carries that away. Someone has to taste the bitterness first, for the ones who come after.

**Xiao Man**: Unlucky for the first one.

**Uncle Wen**: Mm. It is.

**Narrator**: He set the pot down and glanced at the door.

**Narrator**: There were three notches in the doorframe. The lowest was Xiao Man's, her name scratched crookedly beside it. The other two were much higher and cut deep. One said Yan. The other said Chi.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) I asked once whose they were. He burned a whole pot of tea that day. I didn't ask again.

**Narrator**: A knock.

**Narrator**: Three, not soft, not hard, as if the strength had been measured out.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man drew the bolt. Wind and rain poured in. The lamp flame ducked, then stood back up.

**Narrator**: A man stood outside. Straw hat pulled low, rain cape streaming, something long wrapped in oilcloth across his back.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) A sword. However thick you wrap it, that shape is a sword.

**Shen Yan**: Uncle Wen.

**Narrator**: He didn't look at Xiao Man. He looked past her at the old man behind the counter.

**Uncle Wen**: Come in. Floor's wet. Shoes off.

**Narrator**: The man paused, as if that had reminded him of something. He took off his shoes and hung his hat on a nail by the door. Xiao Man had never known what that nail was for.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) Now I do.

**Narrator**: Under the hat, a face of about thirty. High brow, lips pressed tight, like a cut neatly closed.

**Uncle Wen**: Xiao Man. Tea.

**Narrator**: She went back to warm the pot again. The man sat by the window, the bundle across his knees, still wrapped.

**Shen Yan**: Is he here?

**Uncle Wen**: Not yet.

**Shen Yan**: Will he come?

**Uncle Wen**: He will. This is his home.

**Shen Yan**: Not for a long time.

**Narrator**: Her master didn't answer. Xiao Man poured off the first infusion. The man's eyes followed the tea for a moment, then came back.

**Narrator**: The second she evened out in the pitcher and poured into his cup.

**Shen Yan**: Dawn. Threadline Pass.

**Uncle Wen**: I know. I read his letter too.

**Shen Yan**: You won't stop it?

**Uncle Wen**: I tried to stop you two once. What I can't stop, I don't try twice.

**Narrator**: The man held the cup without drinking. Steam climbed between his fingers.

**Shen Yan**: Ten years ago I crossed swords at the pass with the White Egret master's son. He lost. I sheathed my sword and left him breathing.

**Shen Yan**: By dawn he was dead where he lay.

**Shen Yan**: Chi left that night. He took my sword, left his own, and carved one line by the door: "I killed him."

**Narrator**: He said it without any rise or fall, like reading a notice someone else had written.

**Shen Yan**: I'm not here to ask why. Ten years is enough. I'm here to finish it.

**Narrator**: He drank and set the cup down softly. Then, for the first time, he actually looked at her.

**Shen Yan**: And you are?

**Xiao Man**: Xiao Man.

**Shen Yan**: The solar term.

**Uncle Wen**: The day I found her was Xiao Man.

**Shen Yan**: ...The day he found me, he was grinding ink. The day he found Chi, he got there too late.

**Narrator**: He looked at the two notches for a long time. Then he climbed to the loft.

**Narrator**: The bundle stayed on his back the whole way.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) He's carrying someone else's sword.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) For ten years.
