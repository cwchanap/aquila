# Act 2: The Second Guest

**Narrator**: Past midnight, her master went to bed, banking the fire low on his way.

**Uncle Wen**: The last pot before dawn is yours. When it's ready, carry it up to the pass.

**Xiao Man**: For who?

**Uncle Wen**: For whoever's still standing.

**Narrator**: His door closed. The rain eased from pounding to muttering.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man was clearing cups when she noticed one more person at the window table.

**Narrator**: No knock. The bolt was still across the door.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) ...The back window. That latch has been broken for six months.

**Lu Chi**: Don't scream. I'm not a thief. I live here.

**Narrator**: A few years younger than the one upstairs. Soaked hair carelessly raked back, a faint smile, like someone being polite about a joke that wasn't funny.

**Narrator**: A sword at his waist. The sheath was old, and cleaner than anything else on him.

**Lu Chi**: You're the new one he picked up?

**Xiao Man**: Seven years. Not new.

**Lu Chi**: New to me.

**Narrator**: He went to the doorframe and touched the notch marked Chi. His fingertip lingered.

**Lu Chi**: Huh. I was that short.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) It's a head above mine.

**Lu Chi**: Any tea?

**Narrator**: Crab eyes, fish eyes, warm the pot, leaves in. When she poured off the first infusion, he laughed softly behind her.

**Lu Chi**: Still pouring it out. Ten years and he hasn't quit.

**Xiao Man**: It's not a habit. The first infusion tastes the bitterness first, for the ones who come after.

**Lu Chi**: ...He's still saying that.

**Narrator**: He drank in one swallow, winced at the heat, and pushed the cup back.

**Lu Chi**: The one upstairs. What's he carrying?

**Xiao Man**: A sword. Wrapped in oilcloth.

**Lu Chi**: Black sheath. Chipped at the tip.

**Xiao Man**: I didn't see.

**Lu Chi**: That's my sword. This one's his. We've carried each other's for ten years. Funny, isn't it.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) No.

**Narrator**: He saw that. The smile faded a little, and not the rest of the way.

**Lu Chi**: The old man's asleep. My brother's asleep. You're the only one awake.

**Lu Chi**: I'll tell you something. You don't count, so I can.

**Xiao Man**: What does "don't count" mean?

**Lu Chi**: Telling you is the same as telling no one. Like the first infusion.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) He's really annoying.

**Narrator**: But she didn't leave. She sat down.

**Lu Chi**: Ten years ago my brother fought the White Egret master's son at the pass. The man lost. My brother sheathed his sword and walked off without looking back.

**Lu Chi**: I was watching. The man stood up, sat back down, and didn't move again.

**Lu Chi**: When I reached him, the blood had soaked into the cracks in the stone. My brother's thrust went too deep. He didn't know. He's never known how heavy his own sword is.

**Narrator**: Water dripped from the eaves, slow enough to count.

**Lu Chi**: White Egret can read a wound. That width, one look and they know which sword.

**Lu Chi**: He was twenty. The old man had just said the teahouse would be his. I was seventeen and had nothing.

**Lu Chi**: So I took his sword, left mine, and carved that line. That's all.

**Xiao Man**: ...That's all?

**Lu Chi**: That's all. White Egret has chased me ten years. Fair enough. I'm quick on my feet.

**Xiao Man**: Then at dawn—

**Lu Chi**: At dawn he wins.

**Narrator**: He said it lightly, the way you'd say tomorrow would clear up.

**Lu Chi**: White Egret wants a dead killer. If he kills me, that settles it, and they'll owe the house a favor besides. He comes back, takes over this place. Clean.

**Lu Chi**: The old man's getting old. Somebody has to boil the water.

**Xiao Man**: You could tell him.

**Lu Chi**: Then he'd have to carry it. He shoulders other people's mistakes. Never mind one that's really his.

**Lu Chi**: I've carried it ten years. My shoulders have grown into the shape.

**Narrator**: He turned the empty cup a full circle and set it upside down.

**Lu Chi**: All you have to do is pour the tea.

**Lu Chi**: And at dawn, when you pour off the first infusion, don't make that face.

**Narrator**: He leaned against the wall and closed his eyes, as if he could actually sleep.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) The first infusion.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) So that's who he meant.
