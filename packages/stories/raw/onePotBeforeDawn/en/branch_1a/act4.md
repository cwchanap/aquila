# Act 4: Spoken

**Narrator**: She didn't pour it.

**Xiao Man**: That night, when he ran over, the man was already dead.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan raised his eyes.

**Xiao Man**: It was your thrust. It went too deep, and you didn't know. White Egret can read a wound, so he took your sword. That line by the door, he carved it for you.

**Xiao Man**: At dawn he's going to be a little slow. He said that way you could come back clean and boil the water.

**Narrator**: That was all of it. Much shorter than she'd expected. Ten years, and poured out it filled one small cup.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan didn't move.

**Narrator**: His hand was pressed to the sheath. His knuckles went white, slowly, and then slowly let go.

**Shen Yan**: He told you.

**Xiao Man**: Mm.

**Shen Yan**: Why you?

**Xiao Man**: He said I don't count.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan laughed once. Very short, like something snapping in his throat.

**Shen Yan**: Same as ever. He always puts things in the place worth the least.

**Narrator**: He stood, took the sword from his knees, went to the door, and rested his hand on it. He stayed like that a long time.

**Narrator**: Then he pushed it open and went out.

**Narrator**: Lu Chi was standing under the eaves. The two of them stood side by side, facing the valley, one person's width between them.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man could see them. She couldn't hear them.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan said something, very short. Lu Chi didn't turn. After a long while, he said something too.

**Narrator**: Then neither of them spoke again. Water dripped from the eaves, one drop at a time, onto the flagstone between them.

**Narrator**: When the first line of light showed at the edge of the sky, Lu Chi unfastened the sword at his waist and held it out.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan looked down at it for a long time. He took it, and gave back the one with the black sheath.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) Ten years carrying each other's swords. Trading back took one breath.

**Narrator**: Lu Chi turned and went down the stone steps. Not toward Threadline Pass. Down the mountain. The fog took him a piece at a time, first his feet, then his back, then last of all that carelessly raked-back hair.

**Narrator**: He didn't look back. Shen Yan didn't follow.

**Narrator**: Her master's door opened a crack, then quietly closed.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan came back inside, laid the sword on the table, and sat.

**Shen Yan**: Second infusion.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man poured the cold first infusion into the channel and filled the pot again. This time her hands didn't shake.

**Xiao Man**: What did he say?

**Shen Yan**: Not to go to White Egret.

**Xiao Man**: And what did you say?

**Narrator**: Shen Yan lifted his cup and looked at the bottom of it, and didn't answer.

**Narrator**: The sun came up over the pass. Threadline Pass stood empty, the fog lifting slowly, no one there waiting for anyone.

**Narrator**: Both swords stayed in their sheaths.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) I poured tea all night.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) That one line was the only thing nobody poured for me.
