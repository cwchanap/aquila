# Act 4: Threadline Pass

**Narrator**: She poured the first infusion away.

**Narrator**: Amber with a touch of red, it ran into the channel without a sound.

**Narrator**: The second she poured into Shen Yan's cup. He drank it.

**Shen Yan**: Good tea.

**Xiao Man**: ...It's the master's tea.

**Shen Yan**: No. You brewed it.

**Narrator**: He tied the black-sheathed sword at his waist and pushed the door open. The man outside was waiting. They climbed the steps toward the pass one behind the other, without a word.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man filled a small pot with what was left and followed with the tea basket.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) For whoever's still standing.

**Narrator**: Threadline Pass. A path squeezed between two cliffs, and overhead only a thread of sky.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man stood on the steps below. Fog flowed past her feet like a river in no hurry.

**Narrator**: They stopped seven paces apart.

**Lu Chi**: Brother. How does my sword carry?

**Shen Yan**: Badly. Too light.

**Lu Chi**: Yours is too heavy. Ten years, and my shoulders sit crooked.

**Narrator**: Neither drew first. The sun hadn't quite risen.

**Narrator**: Then light spilled in from the far end of the pass and settled on the stones between them.

**Narrator**: The swords cleared their sheaths in a single sound. Two swords, one sound.

**Narrator**: The first exchange touched once and parted, like testing the water.

**Narrator**: The second, Shen Yan's blade shaved a strip from Lu Chi's sleeve. Lu Chi's blade stopped three inches from Shen Yan's throat, and went no further.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) He stopped. He stopped.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan's brow moved, just slightly.

**Narrator**: The third exchange, Lu Chi was slow.

**Narrator**: So obviously slow that even Xiao Man could see it. His sword rose halfway and left his chest open, like a door left unlatched on purpose.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan's sword was already on its way. There was no calling it back.

**Narrator**: It was quiet. Much quieter than she had imagined.

**Narrator**: Lu Chi stepped back and sat down slowly against the cliff. His sword slid onto the stone and rang once.

**Shen Yan**: You—

**Lu Chi**: Your sword's still faster than mine.

**Shen Yan**: You were slow. On purpose.

**Lu Chi**: Mm. Getting old.

**Shen Yan**: You're twenty-seven.

**Lu Chi**: For me, that's old.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan knelt, one hand pressed to his chest. He didn't know where to put the other.

**Lu Chi**: Xiao Man. Any tea?

**Narrator**: She came up and took out a cup. Her hands shook, and half the tea spilled.

**Lu Chi**: Don't make that face.

**Narrator**: He held the cup without drinking, letting the steam climb between his fingers.

**Lu Chi**: Brother. Go back and boil the water. The old man's getting old.

**Shen Yan**: Chi, why—

**Lu Chi**: The tea's gone cold.

**Narrator**: He said nothing else. The cup stayed in his hand. The steam thinned until there was none.

**Narrator**: The sun was fully up. The thread of sky had become all of it.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan knelt a long time. Then he looked up at Xiao Man.

**Shen Yan**: Last night. What did he tell you?

**Narrator**: She opened her mouth.

**Narrator**: The words were right there in her throat, amber with a touch of red.

**Xiao Man**: ...He said the master's tea hasn't changed in ten years.

**Narrator**: Shen Yan nodded, as if that were enough. He lifted Lu Chi onto his back and went down the steps, one sword at his waist, the other in his hand.

**Narrator**: Xiao Man poured the cold cup out onto the stones of the pass.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) The first infusion. Poured away.

**Xiao Man**: (thinking) Someone has to taste the bitterness first, for the ones who come after.
