# Act 2: The Note

**Narrator**: Washer No. 3 finished its song.

**Narrator**: The song had four notes and was sincerely terrible. She crouched down and scooped the wet laundry into her basket, handful by handful.

**Narrator**: Scrubs. Scrubs. T-shirt. Towel. A whole row of black compression socks.

**Yining**: (thinking) So I'd never have to match them, I bought twelve identical pairs at once. The most mature decision of my life.

**Narrator**: Then she pulled out a grey sock.

**Narrator**: Wool, the heel worn so thin it was almost see-through. Not hers. She didn't own anything grey, apart from her complexion.

**Yining**: (thinking) The machine ate someone else's sock and spat it out into my load. Makes sense. This machine has never been picky.

**Narrator**: There was something inside the sock. Something small and stiff.

**Narrator**: She reached in and drew out a tiny zip bag, the kind the hospital pharmacy used to portion out pills. She handled dozens of them a night. Inside was a sheet of paper folded in quarters. Dry.

**Narrator**: She opened it. Blue ballpoint, very neat handwriting, every stroke looking typeset.

**Narrator**: "If you found this, the machine ate my other sock and most of my courage."

**Narrator**: She read it twice.

**Yining**: (thinking) A zip bag.

**Yining**: (thinking) Who seals a note in a waterproof zip bag and then "accidentally" lets the washer eat it? This was no accident. This was premeditated. Very thoughtfully premeditated.

**Narrator**: She didn't look up right away. On the ward she had learned one thing: if you want to know how someone is really doing, don't let them catch you looking.

**Narrator**: She gathered up the wet laundry and carried it to No. 4, watching him from the corner of her eye.

**Narrator**: He was reading his pages. The same page.

**Narrator**: In the four minutes, at least, since she'd crouched down, that page hadn't turned. The red pen was capped. A man who chewed his pen cap while he worked, and the cap was on.

**Narrator**: His ears were very red.

**Yining**: (thinking) Respiratory rate roughly twenty-four a minute. Elevated. Patient states: I'm reading.

**Narrator**: She loaded No. 4, fed it coins, and pressed forty minutes. The drum began to turn, the warm amber light falling across her hand, the note still pinched between her fingers.

**Narrator**: The handwriting looked familiar.

**Narrator**: She turned to the instructions on the wall. "Or people." The same neat, typeset-looking hand, only in red ink.

**Yining**: (thinking) Right. Caught red-handed.

**Narrator**: Before she had worked out what to say, he stood up. He shoved the pages into his backpack so fast it looked like he was fleeing a crime scene, got as far as the door, and stopped. He looked back.

**Red Pen**: I'm... going across the street to get something.

**Red Pen**: I'll leave my clothes here. If... if you need to go, don't worry about them.

**Narrator**: He left his laundry bag on the second chair and pushed out the door. The rain got loud all at once, then the door shut it out again.

**Narrator**: She watched him run across the road, shoulders hunched, into the white glow of the convenience store, and come to a stop in front of the magazine rack.

**Narrator**: His back to her. Standing very straight, perfectly still, like a family member waiting for test results.
