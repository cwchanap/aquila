# Act 4: Writing Back

**Narrator**: She turned the note over.

**Narrator**: The pen hovered for a long time. She had written thousands of nursing notes and never needed to think this long. Alert and oriented, vitals stable, chief complaint: none.

**Narrator**: In the end she wrote: "Sock is with me, condition stable. As for the courage, the machine reports no sighting. Recommend continued observation."

**Yining**: (thinking) ...I wrote it like a shift handover. Oh well.

**Narrator**: She slid the note back into the zip bag, tucked it into the grey sock, and set it on top of his laundry bag. Then she went back to the fourth chair and started folding her clothes. Very, very slowly.

**Narrator**: Five minutes later, the door opened.

**Narrator**: He came in smelling of rain, holding a bottle of hot soy milk that looked like he'd grabbed it at random, because he didn't seem to know why he was holding it either.

**Narrator**: He saw the sock and froze. Sat down. Opened the zip bag. Read. He read for so long she started to wonder if she'd written it in a foreign language.

**Narrator**: Then he uncapped the red pen, wrote something, folded it, and set the sock on the empty chair between them.

**Narrator**: Without a word.

**Yining**: (thinking) ...Okay. So those are the rules.

**Narrator**: She picked it up. "Thank you for admitting it. The family wishes to ask: could the courage be stuck in the rubber seal of No. 3?"

**Yining**: (thinking) He's copying my format. He's a translator. Of course he's copying my format.

**Narrator**: She wrote: "Rubber seal examined. Found one ten-dollar coin and one hair of unknown origin. Courage not located." She put it back on the middle chair.

**Narrator**: When he finished reading, his shoulders shook once. She was certain he was trying not to laugh.

**Narrator**: "Ten dollars buys No. 2 one more spin. Please accept it as your registration fee."

**Narrator**: "This facility does not charge registration fees. However, the family has given up No. 4 every week and left with wet clothes, and this facility has been noting it for some time."

**Narrator**: This time he paused for a long while. When the note came back, his writing was smaller, as if it didn't want to take up too much room.

**Narrator**: "I come here when I can't sleep. Then I noticed I sleep better on Thursdays."

**Narrator**: She read that line three times. At some point, outside, the rain had stopped.

**Narrator**: Drops still clung to the glass. The sky had faded from black to that blue-grey of something washed too many times, and across the street the convenience store light suddenly looked exhausted in the dawn.

**Narrator**: The back of the note was full. She turned it to the front, and in the margin beside the word "courage," she wrote one last line: "Zhou Yining. Evening shift. Thursdays."

**Narrator**: She didn't leave it on the chair. She stood, stepped past the empty seat, and put the note directly into his hand.

**Narrator**: He looked up. Up close, his dark circles were even deeper than she'd thought.

**Red Pen**: ...Jiang Yuan.

**Red Pen**: My name's Jiang Yuan. Sorry, I should have written that first. My handwriting works better than my mouth.

**Yining**: I know. The sign on the wall was you too, wasn't it?

**Red Pen**: It told everyone not to put animals inside. I thought people deserved a mention.

**Yining**: Very thoughtful. As thoughtful as the zip bag.

**Narrator**: His ears went red again. This time, she decided not to count his breathing.

**Narrator**: He held out the bottle of soy milk. It had long since gone lukewarm, like clothes pulled out of No. 4 halfway through.

**Red Pen**: This was meant for you. I just stood over there too long.

**Yining**: Lukewarm is perfect. I can't drink anything too hot after a shift.

**Narrator**: They walked out together. Under the arcade sat two scooters, hers and his, with someone else's parked between them.

**Narrator**: For six months, it turned out, there had always been someone else's between them.

**Red Pen**: Um... the other sock. I'll bring it next Thursday. Make a pair.

**Yining**: Didn't you say the machine ate it?

**Red Pen**: Yes. I'll find a way to make it give it back.

**Narrator**: She laughed out loud. The first time in six months she had laughed out loud in front of him.

**Yining**: Okay. See you Thursday.

**Red Pen**: See you Thursday.

**Narrator**: By the time she rode out from under the arcade, the sky was a little lighter. In her mirror he was still standing at the laundromat door, holding that note covered in writing as if it were a very important manuscript.

**Yining**: (thinking) Next Thursday, he gets No. 4 first.
