# Act 1: The Bustling Platform

**Narrator**: Late night on the Chuo Line platform at Shinjuku Station. The departure board glowed a cold white against the dark, its numbers flicking over with a mechanical indifference. Ten to midnight — the last train bound for Takao was about to pull in.

**Narrator**: The air on the platform hung thick and clammy, heavy with the alcohol people had carried out of the izakayas and the greasy smell of convenience-store fried chicken. The weekend's revels were winding down, and people stood in twos and threes, scattered among the benches and pillars.

**Narrator**: Some dozed against the pillars. Some laughed wearily at their phone screens. Others gazed blankly into the black mouth of the tunnel, as if waiting for some kind of salvation.

**Narrator**: Li Jie stood behind the yellow line, head tipped slightly back, letting the cool night air brush his face. His tie had long since slipped loose, and his open collar showed the tired lines of his neck.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Another loud night... I can still hear the party — the drinks, the laughter. But the moment I turn around, it's just me and this cold platform.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Twenty-eight. Not up, not down; not a success, not a failure. Just like the last train — always stuck somewhere short of the end of the line. I can't tell if that's something to be glad about or something to sigh over.

**Narrator**: He let out a soft sigh, and his breath clouded white in the air and vanished. Li Jie had a habit of watching strangers, guessing at their stories from their faces, the way they stood, the creases in their clothes.

**Narrator**: His gaze swept the platform, and the first thing it caught was a knot of university students, full of life. They were the brightest color in all that gray — laughing with total abandon, gesturing wildly.

**Loudmouth Student**: No, no, listen to me! That place was insane! "Craft beer specialists," they call themselves, and what they brought out tasted like dishwater! I'm telling you, one sip and I almost threw up!

**Loudmouth Student**: And the owner's standing there all smug, like, "Well? Lots of depth, huh?" Depth my ass!

**Bubbly Student**: Yes, yes, yes! It was so gross! And did you see his face? "This one is brewed with hops imported from Belgium" — and then it's eight hundred yen a glass! The Strong Zero we got at FamilyMart hit way harder than that!

**Chatterbox Student**: Right, right! And also, did you notice? The chairs there were sooo uncomfortable, my back is killing me! And the bathroom was tiny, like, I could barely turn around in there!

**Chatterbox Student**: And the staff were so weird, too, giving us that snooty look the whole time, like we just didn't get it...

**Loudmouth Student**: Right?! I'm telling you, places like that are a total scam! "Craft culture," "the art of living" — it's all just an excuse to charge more! Never again! I'd rather go to that izakaya by campus. At least you get real portions there!

**Steady Student**: Well... that's not really fair. Craft beer has its market. That place just wasn't for us. Why don't we try the one I like next time? I took a girl from the year below there once. Nice atmosphere, reasonable prices.

**Loudmouth Student**: Oh? Which one? The one you posted in the LINE group last time?

**Bubbly Student**: Ooh, yeah! I wanna know too! Is it the one over in Shibuya? I think I saw it on Instagram!

**Chatterbox Student**: Oh, I know, I know! Is it the one with the giant projector screen? Where you can request songs? My friend went and said it was super fun! And apparently their chicken wings are amazing, and only five hundred yen a plate! Way cheaper than that last place!

**Narrator**: Just then, a young man in rimless glasses and a carefully coordinated casual blazer sidled up to them. He gave two deliberate coughs, cleared his throat, and cut in with what he clearly imagined was an air of mature self-assurance.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Excuse me, everyone... sorry to interrupt. I couldn't help overhearing, and — well, you can tell you're all still very young. The really good places aren't the kind you just find on Instagram or Google.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: People like me, who are out at the clubs all the time and really know Tokyo nightlife... we know the places with real taste are tucked away down the back alleys. If you ever get the chance, I could take you to a spot only insiders know about. The atmosphere there is... on a level you can't even imagine.

**Narrator**: All four students turned at once, a flicker of surprise and impatience in their eyes. The bubbly girl and the chatterbox traded a look, the corners of their mouths twitching as they fought not to laugh.

**Narrator**: The loudmouth froze for a second, then gave a polite nod. The steady one simply looked at the man in glasses, his face blank, and then looked away.

**Loudmouth Student**: Oh... uh, okay. Thanks...

**Bubbly Student**: (whispering) Psst, who even is this...

**Chatterbox Student**: (whispering) No idea. That's so weird, he just popped up out of nowhere... And his smile is so stiff...

**Narrator**: The man in glasses stayed where he was, holding his stiff smile, waiting for an answer. But a few seconds later the four students were already chatting about something else, leaving him stranded at the edge of their circle.

**Loudmouth Student**: Oh, hey, so that place you were talking about — when are we going? You free next weekend?

**Steady Student**: Sure. I'll book a table.

**Bubbly Student**: Me too, me too! I'm coming!

**Chatterbox Student**: Me too, me too! And we can get dinner first and then go drinking! I know this ramen place near there that's sooo good, the chashu literally melts in your mouth...

**Narrator**: The man's smile slowly set hard, and something embarrassed and resentful crossed his face. He took out his phone as if nothing had happened and bowed his head over it as though scrolling, but his thumb never moved. He clearly wasn't looking at anything. The tips of his ears had turned pink, and a thin sheen of sweat had broken out on his forehead.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Must be nice to be young... All that carefree energy. Even if they'll regret it tomorrow, tonight they're going to burn it all. They're fun to watch, too — one runs the conversation, two pile on, one adds the calm footnotes. I guess that's what youth looks like.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) That guy, though... trying that hard to impress, so desperate to be accepted — it only makes him look shallower. Probably hasn't been out in the working world long. Still anxious to fit in somewhere, with no idea how to go about it.

**Narrator**: Li Jie looked away and let his eyes drift idly along the platform — and then they stopped.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Hm? That girl...

**Narrator**: At the far end from the noisy crowd, in the very corner of a platform bench, sat a girl so quiet she almost melted into the night. She wore a light beige cotton dress whose hem rested softly on her knees, showing slender calves and a pair of clean white canvas sneakers.

**Narrator**: She had on a pair of retro wired earphones, the white cord hanging down her chest and swaying faintly with each breath. Her long hair fell smoothly over her shoulders, catching the platform lights in a soft chestnut sheen.

**Narrator**: A slightly worn sketchbook lay open on her lap, and she was drawing something with a thin pencil, utterly absorbed. Her profile was small and delicate; her lowered lashes cast faint shadows. Her lips were pressed lightly together, and every so often her brow drew into a small frown.

**Narrator**: There was a stillness about her that had nothing to do with her surroundings, as if some invisible barrier sealed her off from the noise of the world. Neither the students' racket nor the announcements of arriving trains could break her concentration.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) To be that focused in a place like this... You don't see that often. She seems... special. That shining hair, that small profile, that look of living in some other world... There's something about her that makes you want to protect her. What am I even thinking...

**Narrator**: Li Jie's eyes lingered on her for a few seconds, then slid casually away, as though he were only looking around the platform. They came to rest on a pillar not far off — and in its shadow stood a man dressed entirely in black.

**Narrator**: He wore a black hooded sweatshirt with the hood pulled down low, hiding most of his face. His hands were in his pockets, and he leaned against the pillar without moving at all, like a lifeless statue. People came and went around him, and no one seemed to notice he was there.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Something about that guy feels off. Why wrap himself up like that? And the way he's standing... He doesn't look like he's waiting for a train. More like he's... watching something. Never mind. I'm probably overthinking it. This city's full of all sorts.

**Narrator**: Just then the platform announcement came on, breaking into his thoughts.

**Announcement**: The last train bound for Takao is now arriving. Please wait behind the yellow line.

**Narrator**: A faint glow began to show in the mouth of the distant tunnel. The headlights cut through the dark, the grind of wheels on rail grew clearer, and one silver-white car after another slid slowly into the station. Its cold metal flanks threw back the platform lights, like a long silver snake.

**Narrator**: The train came smoothly to a stop, and the doors opened with a hiss. Chilled air rolled out of the cars, smelling of disinfectant and old upholstery. The waiting crowd began to shuffle forward, boarding one by one.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Finally... Time to go home.

**Narrator**: Li Jie let the crowd carry him aboard and took an empty seat by the window. He set down his backpack, leaned back, and closed his eyes, exhausted. A few seconds later he opened them and glanced around without thinking — the quiet girl had boarded the same car. She was sitting only a little way off, diagonally across from him.

**Narrator**: Her head was still bowed, her earphones still in, the sketchbook on her knees. Her fingers ran lightly over the page, as if checking the lines she had just drawn. Under the carriage lights her profile looked softer still, her lashes scattering fine shadows across her cheeks.

**Narrator**: The four students piled in noisily too. The loudmouth was still going strong, the bubbly girl kept bursting into bright laughter, the chatterbox rattled off details at their side, and the steady one followed quietly at the back, nodding now and then.

**Narrator**: They found seats in the middle of the car, their laughter still ringing through it. The man in glasses had made a point of walking a few steps behind them, as if he were part of the group, but no one looked back at him. He sat down near the door, took out his phone, and made a show of scrolling intently.

**Narrator**: Yet his eyes kept straying to the students, and something lonely and resentful flickered in them.

**Announcement**: The doors are closing. Please stand clear.

**Narrator**: The doors slid shut with a dull clunk, like the close of some ritual. The train gave a slight shudder and slowly began to move. Outside, the city lights started to slide backward; neon flashed across the glass and blurred, growing ever more distant.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Another ordinary night... another ordinary ride home. Tomorrow I'll wake up, and everything will be the same as always.

**Narrator**: He could not yet imagine what fate this train, carrying him out of the city he knew, was taking him toward. Nor could the passengers who seemed so ordinary just then — the quiet girl, the four exuberant students, the timid man in glasses with his put-on worldliness, and the mysterious man in black.

**Narrator**: Together, they were about to set out on a journey beyond anything any of them could understand.

**Narrator**: The lights in the car flickered once, then steadied. The train plunged into a dark tunnel, and the world outside the windows was swallowed whole. All that was left was the sound of wheels on rails, echoing on and on through the silence.
