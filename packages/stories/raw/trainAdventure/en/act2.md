# Act 2: The Long Tunnel

**Narrator**: The train ran smoothly on, its wheels beating a hypnotic rhythm against the rails — clack-clack, clack-clack, clack-clack. Outside, the lights of the city ran together into a flowing river: neon signs, streetlamps, the warm glow of apartment windows, all blurred into smears of light.

**Narrator**: Inside the car, apart from the occasional burst of laughter from the four students, most of the passengers were resting their eyes or scrolling through their phones. Someone in headphones nodded gently along to music; someone else leaned against the window, half asleep. Everything was so ordinary, so reassuring.

**Loudmouth Student**: Hey, hey, you guys wanna shoot some hoops tomorrow? I got a few of the seniors coming...

**Bubbly Student**: Tomorrow? I wanna sleep till noon... Today totally wore me out...

**Chatterbox Student**: Yeah, yeah, I'm sooo tired too, and tomorrow I have to sort out my notes, we've got an exam next week. The professor said it's gonna be super hard, and it covers, like, everything, and I haven't even started studying...

**Steady Student**: Let's worry about tomorrow tomorrow. Home first.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Finally, a little quiet... What an exhausting weekend. Parties, schmoozing, fake smiles... Sometimes I just want to be alone, with nothing to think about.

**Narrator**: Just then, the entrance to a tunnel appeared ahead — a vast black opening, like a great silent maw. The train ran into it without hesitation.

**Narrator**: The carriage lights brightened automatically, casting a pale white glow. In an instant, everything outside the windows was swallowed by pure darkness. The glass became a mirror, giving back blurred reflections of the passengers — tired faces, empty eyes.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Ah, that tunnel. Usually it's about five minutes before we're out. Good time to rest my eyes...

**Narrator**: Li Jie leaned back in his seat and closed his eyes. The monotonous sounds of the train washed over him like a lullaby: the clack-clack of the wheels, the low hum of the air conditioning, the distant screech of metal on metal. His breathing slowed, and his mind began to drift.

**Narrator**: One minute.

**Narrator**: Two minutes.

**Narrator**: Three minutes.

**Narrator**: Five minutes went by.

**Narrator**: Some part of Li Jie felt there should be light by now — the light at the end of the tunnel, the glow of the city, the familiar night view. But beyond his eyelids, the world stayed dim. There were only the carriage lights, and the endless dark.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Hm? Why aren't we out yet? ...Maybe I'm just extra tired tonight and time's dragging. Should be any second now...

**Narrator**: He didn't open his eyes, only shifted in his seat and went on resting. Another five minutes passed.

**Narrator**: The constant drone of the air conditioning and the rhythmic roar of the train over the rails no longer soothed him. Now they sounded like a curse caught in an endless loop. The noise was too regular, too monotonous, as if time itself had stopped flowing.

**Narrator**: The sense that something was wrong grew stronger and stronger, like something cold crawling up his spine. At last he opened his eyes and took out his phone to check the time and the route.

**Narrator**: The screen lit up, its cold white glare stinging his eyes. The time read 12:15 a.m. He remembered the train pulling out at 11:55, which meant it had been running through the tunnel for a full twenty minutes.

**Narrator**: His eyes moved to the top of the screen. There in the status bar, plain as anything, were the words "No Service." Not a single bar of signal.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Twenty minutes... That's not normal. I've been through this tunnel more times than I can count. Five minutes, tops. And now... And I'm not the only one...

**Narrator**: Meanwhile, at the other end of the car, the calm was shattered.

**Bubbly Student**: Wait... seriously? My phone's got zero signal! It's not even saying "out of range," it says "No Service"! How deep underground are we? I've never seen it do that before...

**Loudmouth Student**: Mine too. Nothing's going through. LINE, Insta, nothing. Yo, isn't this tunnel way too long? Was it always this long? I don't remember this at all.

**Chatterbox Student**: Right, right, it's super weird, and don't you guys feel like... it's been forever? I just checked the time, and it's been twenty minutes! Aren't we supposed to be through this tunnel, like, fast?

**Steady Student**: Twenty minutes. Something's wrong.

**Narrator**: The man in glasses saw his chance to show what he knew, and seized it. He stood up and pushed his glasses up his nose with studied calm.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Everyone, there's no need to panic. This is common knowledge. A tunnel's structure seriously interferes with signal propagation. Electromagnetic waves can't penetrate that much rock and concrete, so it's perfectly normal for your phones to have no signal.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Also, there's often maintenance work on the lines late at night. Trains get rerouted around the work zones, which can add twenty minutes or so to the trip. That's extremely common in railway operations. There's nothing to get worked up about.

**Narrator**: He sounded confident, but fine beads of sweat had already broken out on his forehead. The students exchanged looks, still plainly doubtful. He hadn't convinced them.

**Narrator**: Then a deep, steady voice spoke up, puncturing the "air of expertise" the man in glasses had been working so hard to create.

**Generous Older Man**: Now hold on, son, that's not how it is. I've been riding the Chuo Line for thirty years, ever since I was a young man. I could time this tunnel with my eyes shut — five minutes. Six at the most.

**Generous Older Man**: Never — not once — have I been in this tunnel more than ten minutes. This... isn't right.

**Narrator**: His words fell like a stone into still water, sending ripples outward. The passengers who had still been hoping for the best lost the last trace of ease from their faces.

**Anxious Wife**: Honey... I'm getting scared... Didn't you say you knew this line really well? Wh-what's happening? Has something gone wrong?

**Irritable Husband**: How should I know?! I'm not the driver! What's the point of asking me?! God, you're driving me crazy, asking and asking and asking!

**Anxious Wife**: Wh-why are you snapping at me like that...

**Narrator**: Not far away, a little boy's voice piped up, small and full of fear.

**Innocent Little Brother**: Mommy... I'm scared of the dark... It's all dark outside... When are we gonna get out? I wanna go home...

**Gentle Mother**: Soon, sweetheart, soon. Be a good boy. Look, your sister isn't scared at all. Don't look out the window. Why don't we watch some cartoons on my phone, okay?

**Narrator**: She took out her phone, but the screen said "Unable to load."

**Gentle Mother**: Oh... the phone too...

**Narrator**: Her voice broke off, and the color drained from her face. Sensing his mother's fear, the boy cried harder. All she could do was press his face to her chest so he wouldn't see the fear on the faces around them.

**Narrator**: Anxiety spread through the car like a contagious virus. People began muttering to one another, and the whispers turned into open complaints and questions. Some kept checking their watches, some refreshed their phones over and over, some got up and paced.

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: Hey! Something's wrong, obviously! Somebody hit the emergency call button! Or are we just gonna sit here stuck till morning? What if something happens?!

**Generous Older Man**: Calm down! The train's still running smoothly. No strange vibrations — that means the track and the systems are fine. If you go pushing the emergency button and it triggers an emergency stop, we'll be stuck in the middle of the tunnel, and that's when it gets dangerous! We wouldn't even be able to find the emergency exit!

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: Then what do you suggest?! We just sit here and wait?!

**Narrator**: Their quarrel only made the air in the car heavier. Someone began to sob quietly; someone clutched a bag tight; someone else stared dully at the endless dark beyond the windows.

**Narrator**: In all the confusion, Li Jie's eyes went, almost of their own accord, back to the quiet girl. She might have been in another dimension. None of the commotion around her touched her at all.

**Narrator**: She wasn't looking at her phone or out the window, and she showed no sign of panic or unease. She just sat there quietly, absorbed in the sketchbook on her lap, her pencil gliding across the page.

**Narrator**: Li Jie narrowed his eyes, trying to make out what she was drawing. It wasn't a person, or a landscape. It was a strange, totem-like figure built from intricate spirals, jagged angles, and twisting lines.

**Narrator**: The lines seemed almost alive, coiling around and through one another to form an unsettling symmetry. It looked like some ancient symbol — or like writing in a language that should not exist in this world.

**Narrator**: Her hand never paused, as if the patterns had long been carved somewhere deep in her mind and her hand was only copying them onto the paper.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) At a time like this... she's still drawing? And what is that... What even is that? Those symbols... why do they look so eerie? It's like she... doesn't belong here at all. Like she already knew this was going to happen...

**Narrator**: Just as the mood in the car grew heavier and heavier, arguing and crying and questioning all tangled together — up ahead, at the far end of the dark, a thread of light finally seeped through.

**Steady Student**: Ah! Light! Look! We're finally getting out!

**Narrator**: That joyful shout was like a reprieve, and every argument stopped. All eyes turned, as one, toward the growing light. People stood; people crowded to the windows; some wore the smiles of survivors who had just escaped disaster.

**Bubbly Student**: That scared me half to death... We're finally getting out...

**Loudmouth Student**: See? Told you! It was just a detour!

**Chatterbox Student**: Right, right, I said it was fine, it was just a detour, you know? That totally freaked me out just now, I thought something bad was gonna happen, thank god, thank god...

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: See? I told you. Common knowledge...

**Narrator**: But soon, everyone's expressions slid from hope to confusion. There was something... strange about that light.

**Narrator**: It wasn't the familiar warm orange glow of a city at night. It wasn't the white of streetlamps, or the riot of neon. It was... a cold, deep, eerie blue-black haze.

**Narrator**: Like a starless night sky, heavy with the hush of a world where nothing lived — waiting for them, silent and patient.

**Narrator**: The light drew nearer, grew brighter. But inside the car, it only grew quieter. Every smile froze, and in its place came a raw, primal fear.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) ...What is that light? That's not any world I know. What... is that?

**Narrator**: The train pressed on, heading straight into that eerie light without hesitation. The wheels were as monotonous as ever; the air conditioning hummed as steadily as ever. But now the sound was no lullaby. It was a funeral dirge.
