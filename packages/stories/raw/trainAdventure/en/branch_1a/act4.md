# Act 4: The Choice on the Platform

**Narrator**: The air inside the car had gone still as stone. Beyond the open doors lay a night as deep as ink, and from Jakkai Station a cold, unfamiliar presence came seeping in. Every passenger sat frozen, incapable of any response.

**Anxious Wife**: What do we do... what do we do... what are we supposed to do...

**Irritable Husband**: Stop asking me! I don't know either!

**Innocent Little Brother**: Mommy... I'm scared...

**Gentle Mother**: Don't be scared, don't be scared... Mommy's right here...

**Narrator**: Only the sound of stifled breathing remained in the car, along with the faint sound of waves breaking on the shore far away. Someone gripped an armrest for dear life; someone kept their head down, not daring to look out the window; someone else hugged a backpack tight, trembling.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) If we stay here, we're just waiting to die. The train isn't moving anymore, and this is definitely nowhere in Tokyo. If this is a trap, staying inside just makes us sitting ducks. If there's some clue outside... at least there's a chance to look for it.

**Narrator**: Li Jie took a deep breath and forced down the fear in his chest. He knew the decision was an enormous gamble, but something rational in him insisted — between the known and the unknown, right now it was the unknown that might hold a way to live.

**Narrator**: Before he made up his mind, his gaze swept, almost on its own, across the passengers around him. The terrified university students, the husband and wife clinging to each other and shivering, the mother pressing her hands over her child's eyes — and the timid guy in glasses, putting on a calm front with a face white as paper.

**Bubbly Student**: Are we... are we going to die here...

**Loudmouth Student**: Don't say that... There's gotta be a way...

**Chatterbox Student**: But what are we supposed to do? There's no signal, and outside is that creepy place, and there are two moons in the sky — this isn't Earth at all, is it? Where even are we...

**Steady Student**: Calm down... Everyone, calm down...

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Hm? That man...

**Narrator**: Li Jie's eyes stopped on the spot where the man in black had been sitting. The silent, mysterious man who had kept the brim of his cap pulled low was gone! His seat was empty, as if no one had ever sat there. Not a trace had been left behind.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) When... did he get off? Or... was he never there at all?

**Narrator**: The discovery sent a chill through Li Jie, but there was no time to think it over. He stood up.

**Li Jie**: I'm getting off. Staying here won't do any good.

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: What? Are you crazy?

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Getting off? Do you have any idea what's out there?

**Generous Older Man**: Son, don't be rash! We don't know the first thing about what's out there. What if it's dangerous? Better to wait for the conductor... Maybe someone will come along in a bit...

**Li Jie**: I can't wait, mister. Can't you see it? This isn't the world we know. There are two moons in the sky, and outside there's a black sea and a forest straight out of a nightmare. There can't be a conductor here.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Hey now, my good man, if you want to play the hero, at least pick your moment. Don't go imagining yourself so brave — there could be monsters out there, and you'll be the first one eaten! Don't come crying to us after.

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: That's right... Why go running off... What if you lead something back in here...? Are you trying to get us all killed?

**Irritable Husband**: Exactly! If you want to go, go by yourself! Don't drag us into it! We're at least safe on the train!

**Generous Older Man**: You can't talk like that... The young man's only trying to find a way out...

**Anxious Wife**: But... but what if it really is dangerous out there... We have children...

**Loudmouth Student**: Then... then what do we do? Just stay here? Twenty years? Until we starve?

**Steady Student**: Maybe... maybe he's right. Staying here isn't a solution either. We have no food, no water, and who knows how long the power on this train will last.

**Bubbly Student**: But... it's so scary out there... that sea... those trees...

**Li Jie**: There are no answers here. At the very least, I want to go out and see.

**Narrator**: Li Jie hesitated no longer. He picked up his backpack and stepped past the older man.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Hey! You're really going through with it? You'll regret this!

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: A madman... He's out of his mind...

**Narrator**: Li Jie set off toward the open doors. Every step felt like walking a tightrope, every step loaded with uncertainty. Then his feet came down on the cold, rough concrete of the station platform, and reality rushed up to meet him.

**Narrator**: The platform floor was crisscrossed with cracks, moss growing up out of every seam. Underfoot came a gritty crunch — a mix of gravel and dirt. The air carried a heavy salt tang, and with it the smell of rotting wood.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) This place is even more run-down than I imagined...

**Narrator**: He looked around. The platform was short, lit only by a few old lanterns swaying, their light falling on slabs of gray-white stone. At the far end of the platform ran a narrow dirt path into the dark forest; the trees on either side loomed like enormous black shadows, made all the more unnatural by the violet moonlight.

**Narrator**: On the other side of the platform stood a crude waiting room. It had no glass in its windows — nothing but plank walls, seamed with cracks and blotched with mold — and inside it was pitch-black, impossible to make anything out.

**Narrator**: At the entrance of the waiting room he saw a mottled old timetable. He walked over and studied it. The sea wind had eaten it into a patchwork ruin, its lettering all but blurred away, but he could still just make it out — only a single lonely line, leading to some place marked "the City of the Netherworld."

**Narrator**: Every other departure had been worn away by the sea wind; only one line could still be read — [Next train: 15 September 2045, 23:50]. Twenty years to the minute after he had boarded that last train.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) In twenty years... What does that mean? Are we trapped here?

**Narrator**: He turned to look back at the train. The doors were still open.

**Generous Older Man**: Son! What did you find?

**Li Jie**: There's... there's a timetable here. It says the next train won't come for twenty years.

**Loudmouth Student**: What?! Twenty years?! You've gotta be kidding!

**Bubbly Student**: No way... Twenty years...

**Chatterbox Student**: Twenty years? Then what are we supposed to do? Are we supposed to stay here for twenty years? I still have school, and exams, and I have to find a job — how could anyone stay here for twenty years...

**Irritable Husband**: That's a lie! It has to be a lie! Twenty years — how could that be?!

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Didn't I say it! Getting off is a death sentence! Look — even the next train isn't coming for twenty years! We're all going to die here!

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: Get back here! Get back on the train! Maybe it'll move again!

**Narrator**: Li Jie stood alone on the platform, in the light of the two violet moons. The wind passed through, bringing a lingering chill and the sound of waves beating the shore far away. He was just about to head for the waiting room when a small footstep sounded behind him.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Who else is there?

**Narrator**: He turned with a start. The girl who had been sketching on the platform was standing in the train doorway. Her face was still pale, her body trembling slightly, both hands clenching the hem of her skirt. Her eyes shone with fear and tears, like a startled fawn's.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: Hey! What do you think you're doing, girl?!

**Generous Older Man**: Miss, don't be rash!

**Bubbly Student**: Huh... Is she getting off too...?

**Narrator**: The girl didn't answer. Slowly, carefully, one step at a time, she stepped down out of the car and stood on the platform.

**Irritable Husband**: She's lost it! They've all lost it!

**Anxious Wife**: Such a young girl... Won't she be in danger...?

**Gentle Mother**: Miss... you...

**Narrator**: She stood on the platform with her head bowed, not daring to look at Li Jie, nor at the darkness around her. She clutched her sketchbook so hard that her knuckles were white.

**Quiet Girl**: Um... may I... come with you...?

**Narrator**: Her voice was thin as a mosquito's whine, thick with tears, barely audible.

**Loudmouth Student**: She... she actually got off...

**Steady Student**: Maybe... maybe we should too...

**Chatterbox Student**: But... but it's so scary out there... I can't... What if there's something out there...

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: Don't go! Stay on the train! Don't you dare get off! Mark my words, those two will come to no good!

**Narrator**: In the light of the two violet moons she looked so fragile, as if the wind might knock her over at any moment. And yet she gathered her courage and took that step.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) She... chose to get off too?

**Narrator**: Li Jie looked at this girl who seemed so utterly frail, and a complicated feeling rose in his chest. Inside the car, the other passengers still hadn't moved. They only watched, and waited, and were afraid.

**Generous Older Man**: Son... you two... take care of yourselves...

**Narrator**: On the platform there were only Li Jie and this girl. And that endless darkness, waiting quietly for them.
