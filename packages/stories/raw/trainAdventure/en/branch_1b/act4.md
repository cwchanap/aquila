# Act 4: The Sealed Car

**Narrator**: Beyond the open doors lay a bottomless silence, like a gaping mouth waiting for its prey. Cold air kept pouring in from outside, thick with salt and the scent of unfamiliar plants.

**Narrator**: The passengers sat as if a spell had frozen them in place; no one dared to take the first step. The two violet crescent moons cast an eerie light through the windows, turning every face an ashen gray.

**Narrator**: Fear had every one of them by the throat, and time crept by in a suffocating silence.

**Narrator**: Ten minutes passed. Twenty. Thirty. The car stayed deathly still; no one dared to move, no one dared to speak. There was only the occasional sob, the sound of quick, shallow breathing.

**Narrator**: After nearly an hour, Li Jie could no longer bear the torment of waiting.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) We can't just keep waiting like this. We know nothing about what's going on, and staying here is just sitting around waiting to die. At the very least... I need to find out what's wrong with this train itself.

**Narrator**: In the dead silence, Li Jie slowly rose to his feet. That one simple movement drew every eye at once, like a stone dropped into still water.

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: (on his guard) H-hey, you... what are you doing? You're not thinking of going out there alone, are you?

**Li Jie**: (calmly) I want to check the other cars and get a sense of the whole train. See if there are any other passengers, or any crew.

**Timid Guy in Glasses**: (pushing up his glasses, sneering) Hmph. "Get a sense of the whole train"? Looks to me like you just want to play the hero. What's the point of acting so cool at a time like this? Staying right here is the safest thing to do.

**Li Jie**: (coolly) Think what you like.

**Narrator**: Ignoring their jeers, Li Jie turned and headed for the gangway to the car behind. His footsteps rang out sharply in the hushed car.

**Generous Older Man**: (steadily) Be careful, son. If anything looks off, come straight back.

**Li Jie**: (nodding) I will.

**Narrator**: The automatic door at the end of the car slid open with a hiss. An even colder draft struck him in the face. Li Jie took a deep breath, stepped across the gap between the cars, and walked into the next one.

**Narrator**: The second car. Empty. The seats sat in neat rows under the faint white glow of the ceiling lights. A few advertising flyers lay scattered on the floor, stirring gently with the train's faint tremors.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) No one... Not a single person.

**Narrator**: He kept going. The third car. Still empty. Only the low hum of the air conditioning hung in the air. The seats bore traces of the people who had sat there — a forgotten plastic bag, a magazine lying open, an empty coffee can.

**Narrator**: It was as if the passengers had only stepped away for a moment and might be back at any time. But Li Jie knew they were never coming back.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) This is way too strange... Even on the last train, there's no way it'd be just the dozen or so of us. That's far too few passengers...

**Narrator**: A chill crept up Li Jie's spine. But he didn't stop; he decided to keep heading for the front of the train. The fourth car. The fifth. All the same — empty, silent, nothing but the sound of his own footsteps.

**Narrator**: When he reached the fifth car, he glanced out the window without thinking. And in that instant, by the faint light of the two violet crescent moons, he saw it —

**Narrator**: A blurred figure, leaving the platform. It walked toward the deep forest in the distance without once looking back. It was too far away and too dark for him to make out who it was.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Someone got off! Who? Someone from our car?

**Narrator**: Li Jie's heart clenched. Whoever it was, they were the only one who had left the train — someone who might know something, might know where to go. They were the only lead he had.

**Li Jie**: (muttering) Wait!

**Narrator**: Without another moment's hesitation, Li Jie spun around and ran for the nearest door. He reached for the button beside it. Just as his fingertips were about to touch it —

**Narrator**: "Hssss—"

**Narrator**: "Slam!"

**Narrator**: Every door on the entire train slammed shut at the same moment. The heavy crash echoed through every car, like some kind of proclamation. Li Jie's hand froze in midair. Through the glass, he could only watch helplessly as the figure walked farther and farther away.

**Narrator**: Smaller and smaller.

**Narrator**: Until at last it vanished into the darkness of the forest.

**Li Jie**: (pounding on the door) Damn it...

**Narrator**: He jabbed the button hard. Once. Twice. Three times. The doors didn't budge. The emergency release gave no response either.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Locked... Every single door is locked.

**Narrator**: The car had become a cage once more, sealed off from the world. The world outside was close enough to touch, and utterly out of reach. Li Jie leaned against the door, staring at the dark forest that had swallowed his only lead.

**Narrator**: A deep sense of helplessness welled up inside him.

**Li Jie**: (letting out a slow breath) ...Better head back. At least I can tell the others what's going on.

**Narrator**: He turned and started back the way he had come. The empty cars seemed even more sinister now. At some point the lights overhead had begun to flicker faintly, with a thin electrical buzz.

**Narrator**: Li Jie drew a deep breath and set off back down the train. He had to tell the others — every door was locked, someone had already left the train, and they were trapped here.

**Narrator**: He hurried through the empty cars and made it back to where the passengers were gathered. The doors were still shut tight; violet moonlight still spilled in through the windows.

**Narrator**: And far off, the dark forest was still waiting, silent and patient.
