# Act 5: Diverging Journeys

**Narrator**: Li Jie hurried through one empty car after another, his footsteps sharp in the silence. He had to get back and tell the others as fast as he could — every door was locked, and someone had left the train.

**Narrator**: Just as he was passing through the third car, a low hum rose from the whole train, like some great beast stirring from a long sleep.

**Li Jie**: (stopping in his tracks) What's...

**Narrator**: The car began to shake. Li Jie felt the floor sway slightly beneath his feet, and the sound of machinery came to life around him.

**Narrator**: The train was moving again.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Oh no!

**Narrator**: Outside the window, the lonely platform of Jakkai Station began to slide away behind them. The ancient station sign grew smaller and smaller in the violet moonlight until it disappeared into the dark. The train did not enter a tunnel; it kept running along the coast.

**Narrator**: The view outside was as eerie as ever — to the left, the fathomless dark forest; to the right, the black sea, smooth as a mirror. The two violet crescent moons hung in the sky, staining the whole world a sinister violet-black.

**Narrator**: The uncanny scenery streamed endlessly past, a reminder to everyone aboard that they were still in the otherworld, still on a journey into the unknown.

**Narrator**: Li Jie picked up his pace at once and dashed for the gangway. He had to get back — had to tell the others the train was moving again.

**Narrator**: He raced through the remaining empty cars and at last made it back to where the passengers had gathered.

**Narrator**: But the sight in front of him made his heart sink.

**Li Jie**: (stunned) What...

**Narrator**: There were clearly fewer people in the car. The steady older man was gone. The four noisy university students were gone. The smart-aleck guy in glasses was gone. And the quiet girl who had been sketching was gone too.

**Narrator**: A car that had held more than a dozen people now held only a handful. The middle-aged couple still sat in the corner, the selfish middle-aged man leaned by the window, and there was the mother, holding her children close — a son and a daughter.

**Li Jie**: (urgently, to the middle-aged couple) What just happened? Where is everyone?

**Irritable Husband**: (impatient, but with a note of relief) What do you think happened? That older guy saw you go poking around up front and couldn't sit still either. Said something about how we couldn't just sit here waiting to die — he wanted to get off and check out the platform, look for clues.

**Anxious Wife**: (jumping in, talking fast) Yes, yes, and then that quiet girl stood up too and said, really softly, that she'd go along and help. And that young man in the glasses probably wanted to show off, so he went too.

**Irritable Husband**: (scornfully) And those college kids, don't even get me started. Yelling about how "it's like a big adventure, it's so exciting!" and off they ran, laughing their heads off. A bunch of brats who think nothing can touch them.

**Anxious Wife**: We tried to talk them out of it! I told them it was too dangerous out there, but they just wouldn't listen...

**Li Jie**: (after a few seconds of silence) ...I see.

**Narrator**: Li Jie looked around. Every one of the people left wore an unmistakable look of relief. To them, the train starting up again seemed like a sign of salvation.

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: (gloating) Hmph, good riddance. Much more peaceful now. That old guy kept bossing everybody around, and those students were so damn loud. I bet this train's heading back now!

**Gentle Mother**: (holding her child tight, her voice trembling but full of hope) Thank goodness... The train's moving. Does this... does this mean we can go home?

**Innocent Little Brother**: (looking up) Mommy, can we really go home?

**Gentle Mother**: (stroking his hair) Yes, sweetie, we can...

**Narrator**: Quiet cheers and murmurs of relief rose around the car. The selfish middle-aged man even cracked a smile, and the anxious wife's face relaxed a great deal. Everyone was lost in the false hope that the train was moving again and they were about to "go home."

**Narrator**: Only Li Jie couldn't feel the slightest bit of relief.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Home? ...This train spent twenty minutes in that tunnel and brought us into this world. It stopped at Jakkai, opened its doors, then slammed them shut out of nowhere... Is all of that really just a coincidence?

**Narrator**: The image of that blurred figure walking alone into the dark forest kept rising in Li Jie's mind. And the people who had gotten off — the steady older man, the quiet girl, the four lively young people.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Who was that walking into the forest? One of the people who just got off? Or... "someone" who was never one of us in the first place?

**Narrator**: He turned to the window. The train kept running along the coastline, its speed smooth and unchanging. The black sea glinted an eerie silver in the moonlight, and the deep forest ran on and on like a black wall.

**Narrator**: No city lights. No familiar station signs. No sign at all of the way home.

**Li Jie**: (quietly, so only he could hear) Are we really going home... or...

**Narrator**: A heavy unease hung over Li Jie like a storm cloud. But there was nothing he could do. Shut inside this steel box racing along the coast of another world, all he could do was wait.

**Narrator**: Wait for whatever came next.

**Narrator**: The train rolled on. The sound of the wheels on the rails echoed through the silence — clack, clack, clack. Like a countdown. Or the slow beat of a funeral march.

**Narrator**: Outside, the two violet moons watched the lonely train in silence, like a pair of cold eyes.
