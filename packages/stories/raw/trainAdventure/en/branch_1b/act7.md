# Act 7: The Abandoned Junction

**Narrator**: Li Jie stepped out onto the platform of the City of the Netherworld. The flagstones beneath his feet were cold and hard, webbed with the cracks of long ages.

**Narrator**: The shrill arguing in the car behind him was cut off as if by an invisible wall, replaced in an instant by the deathly silence of the platform.

**Narrator**: The air was cold and thin, with a smell like damp earth mixed with rusting metal. The two violet moons hung in the sky, staining the whole platform an eerie violet-black.

**Narrator**: But just then, an even fiercer argument broke out in the car behind him.

**Irritable Husband**: (roaring with anger) It's cowards like you! Back at Jakkai you didn't have the guts to even fart! And now all you can do is scream your head off!

**Selfish Middle-Aged Man**: (shrilly) What's that got to do with me? If you're so tough, you get off! And that guy who went off on his own is an idiot too, if you ask me — running off by himself to get killed!

**Irritable Husband**: (rounding on the other side) And you! Keep your kids under control! Crying, crying, crying — it's driving me nuts!

**Steady Father**: (shielding his family behind him, firing back angrily) What are you yelling for?! The kids are scared, so they cry — so what? If you've got time to stand here cursing people out, try thinking of a way to get us out of here!

**Narrator**: The quarrel in the car grew worse and worse. Fear and despair drove them to tear at each other, each one desperate for somewhere to vent.

**Narrator**: Li Jie paid no attention to their pointless bickering. He turned and began to take a careful look at the platform.

**Narrator**: The platform was vast and utterly deserted. Ancient stone pillars held up the soaring roof, and the walls were carved all over with symbols he couldn't read. A thick layer of dust covered the ground, without a single footprint — except the ones he had just left.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) No one's been here... in a very long time.

**Narrator**: His eyes came to rest on a huge sign in the middle of the platform. It was made of dark, heavy wood, and in the light of the violet moons it gave off an ominous air.

**Narrator**: Carved across the top were four large characters — "City of the Netherworld." Below them, the directions were clearly marked.

**Narrator**: Beside the arrow pointing back the way the train had come were the characters "Jakkai." And beside the arrow pointing onward were the words "Canyon of the Dead."

**Li Jie**: (reading aloud, softly) Jakkai... the Canyon of the Dead...

**Narrator**: The writing carved there wasn't printed type but an ancient script full of sharp, jagged strokes. Each character looked as if it had been gouged out stroke by stroke with some sharp tool, full of a rough, primal force.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Just as I thought... this line doesn't end at Shinjuku. Getting home on this train isn't going to happen.

**Narrator**: Just as despair was creeping over him, he noticed a smaller board beside the sign, carved from the same black wood.

**Narrator**: It seemed to be some kind of timetable. There were only three lines on it. He couldn't read the words, but the years after them were written in Arabic numerals he knew well.

**Narrator**: Previous train: 2005

**Narrator**: This train: —

**Narrator**: Next train: 2045

**Li Jie**: (stunned) 2005... 2045...?

**Narrator**: Li Jie's heart sank like a stone. It was 2025. The previous train had come twenty years ago; the next would come twenty years from now.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Twenty years apart... A train only comes through this station once every twenty years?

**Narrator**: A chill shot from the soles of his feet to the top of his head. He looked around at the crumbling buildings, the rust-eaten fixtures — and it all made sense.

**Narrator**: The station was so derelict because a train might come through only once every twenty years. And their arrival seemed to be an accident that never should have happened.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) If I stay here and wait for the next train... that's twenty years from now. "Waiting" is a dead end, no two ways about it.

**Narrator**: The discovery settled it — he had to find a way out that didn't depend on the train. He turned and headed for the main station building.

**Narrator**: The moment he stepped into the waiting hall, his footsteps rang out in clear echoes through the cavernous space. Every step seemed to remind him how empty this place was, how dead.

**Narrator**: The hall was enormous, its ceiling so high he could barely make it out. The light of the two violet moons fell through grimy arched windows high above, casting warped, eerie patches of light on the dusty floor.

**Narrator**: There were cobwebs everywhere. Thick strands of spider silk hung from the ceiling, glinting silvery white in the faint light. Fine motes of dust drifted slowly through the beams.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) How long has this place been abandoned...

**Narrator**: He went around behind a counter at one side of the hall. It was covered in a thick layer of dust, and on it lay an open register.

**Narrator**: The pages had long since gone yellow and brittle, their edges starting to crumble. They were filled with indecipherable symbols in the same jagged script, like some ancient language.

**Narrator**: Li Jie turned the pages carefully, but the writing was completely alien to him. He gave up trying to read it and kept exploring.

**Narrator**: He passed through rows of ticket gates rusted solid. They hadn't turned in ages, their metal surfaces mottled with reddish-brown rust.

**Narrator**: But one gate caught his attention — it had been violently smashed, twisted out of shape and left hanging crookedly open. The metal bore obvious marks of impact.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Did someone... force their way through this? What happened here?

**Narrator**: Everything in the station pointed to a single fact — this place had been abandoned for an extraordinarily long time. And before that, it seemed, there had been some kind of turmoil.

**Narrator**: In one corner of the hall, he found a room that looked like the stationmaster's office. The door stood ajar; it wasn't locked.

**Narrator**: He pushed it open. The hinges gave a shrill creak, jarringly loud in the silence. A heavier smell of mildew hit him in the face, mixed with the odor of rotting paper.

**Narrator**: The room was a wreck. Tables and chairs overturned, papers scattered across the floor, wooden panels peeling off the walls to expose the mottled plaster beneath.

**Narrator**: But one spot on the wall, protected by a cracked glass frame, caught his eye. It was the only thing in the room still intact.

**Li Jie**: (stepping closer, softly) This is...

**Narrator**: He brushed away the dense cobwebs and carefully removed the broken glass. Underneath was a yellowed map.

**Narrator**: It was finely drawn; though the paper had yellowed, the lines were still perfectly legible. With this station at its center, three main routes were clearly marked.

**Narrator**: South, to the Misty Forest. North, toward the Canyon of the Dead. And west, to Silver Moon Lake.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) Three routes... At least there's a choice.

**Narrator**: Without hesitation, Li Jie took the precious map down from the wall. He folded the yellowed paper carefully and slipped it into his pocket.

**Narrator**: He kept searching the station. Beside a passage that led to an exit, sealed off by a massive iron grille, stood a guardroom with its door half open.

**Narrator**: He went in and found a sheet-metal locker that had been kicked in. Its door hung crookedly, and inside there was nothing but a few tattered papers and some rotting cloth.

**Narrator**: But in the corner behind the locker, he spotted something long and slender — a sword, leaning against the wall.

**Li Jie**: (surprised) A sword?

**Narrator**: He bent down and picked it up. The blade was covered in rust and its edge had long since dulled, but it still felt heavy and solid in his hand.

**Narrator**: The hilt was wrapped in rotting strips of cloth, but it sat well enough in his grip. This was a weapon someone had once used.

**Li Jie**: (thinking) In a godforsaken place like this, something is better than nothing.

**Narrator**: Gripping the sword, he felt a faint, uncertain sense of security. At least he was no longer empty-handed.

**Narrator**: Li Jie meant to go back to the car first and tell the others what he had found. But just as he stepped out of the station hall —

**Narrator**: Two small figures came running out of the car, crying.

**Sensible Big Sister**: (running to Li Jie, her eyes red) Mister! Please, you have to come and see!

**Innocent Little Brother**: (close to tears, tugging at Li Jie's clothes) My daddy... My daddy's fighting that fat man!

**Li Jie**: (frowning) What?

**Sensible Big Sister**: (crying) They were yelling really bad, and then they started shoving each other... and Mom's crying too...

**Narrator**: Li Jie looked at the two children's terrified faces, and heard the furious shouting and the thuds coming from the car.

**Li Jie**: (sighing) ...All right. Wait here, both of you.

**Narrator**: Gripping the sword, he strode quickly toward the car.
